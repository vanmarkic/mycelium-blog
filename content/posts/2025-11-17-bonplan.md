## Introduction

**Le Syndicat des Tox** creates a judgment-free discussion space for individuals struggling with substance dependency in Belgium. The platform emphasizes complete anonymization, accessibility on low-bandwidth connections, and integration with local crisis resources.

This isn't just another forum—it's a carefully architected privacy-preserving space where security and anonymity are foundational, not features.

## The Story

### Context: What I Was Building

People in addiction situations face stigma, isolation, and barriers to accessing support. Traditional forums require email registration, log IP addresses, and create digital paper trails that users fear. Judgment from family, employers, or law enforcement makes anonymity critical—not optional.

**Le Syndicat des Tox** provides a completely anonymous peer support platform where users authenticate with pseudonyms and simple PIN codes. No email. No phone numbers. No IP logging. No tracking. The platform runs on older devices with slow connections—because digital exclusion shouldn't compound social exclusion.

The technical foundation:
- **Node.js + Express.js** backend with session-based authentication
- **MariaDB 10.11+** for data storage
- **Redis 7+** for session caching
- **Nginx** reverse proxy with Cloudflare CDN
- **Belgian hosting** with zero external dependencies

### The Challenge

**Anonymity vs. abuse prevention.** Without email verification or IP logging, how do you prevent spam, harassment, or abuse? Traditional anti-abuse measures (rate limiting by IP, email bans, device fingerprinting) compromise anonymity. The challenge: protect community safety without sacrificing privacy.

**GDPR compliance without data.** GDPR mandates user rights (data export, deletion, correction). But what if you deliberately don't collect user data? How do you implement "right to be forgotten" when you never remembered in the first place? The solution: GDPR compliance through data minimization.

**Accessibility on low-spec devices.** Many users access from older smartphones with limited RAM and slow connections. Rich modern web frameworks (React, Vue, heavy JavaScript) are exclusionary. The platform must work on 5-year-old Android phones over 3G connections.

**Multi-language support for Belgium.** Belgium has French, Dutch, German, and English-speaking communities. Support content, crisis resources, and moderation guidelines need accurate translation—but legal/medical terminology varies regionally.

**Community moderation without centralized power.** Traditional forums have admin/moderator hierarchies with escalating privileges. This creates power dynamics that can harm vulnerable users. The challenge: enable community self-moderation with minimal privilege escalation.

### How I Solved It

**Pseudonym + PIN authentication.** Users create pseudonyms with simple PIN codes. No email, no password complexity requirements. Authentication uses **Argon2id** hashing—industry-standard password hashing resistant to GPU attacks. Sessions persist in Redis with aggressive expiration.

**Zero IP logging.** The platform deliberately doesn't log IP addresses. Not in application logs, not in access logs, not in analytics. Nginx is configured to drop IP data before it reaches application code. This is architecture-level privacy, not configuration-level.

**Rate limiting without IP tracking.** Instead of rate limiting by IP, rate limiting applies per session. Aggressive session timeouts prevent abuse. If a session creates spam, it gets terminated—but IP blocking never happens.

**GDPR through minimization.** The platform implements GDPR by not collecting regulated data. No emails, no IP addresses, no personally identifiable information. User pseudonyms and post content are deletable on request, satisfying GDPR's "right to erasure."

**Server-side rendering with minimal JavaScript.** The platform uses **EJS templates** for server-side rendering. JavaScript is minimal and progressive—features degrade gracefully on older browsers. This keeps the platform accessible on low-spec devices.

**Aggressive Content Security Policy (CSP).** CSP headers prevent XSS attacks by restricting inline scripts, external resources, and unsafe eval. Security isn't just about authentication—it's about preventing exploitation at every layer.

**Self-hosted infrastructure.** All components (MariaDB, Redis, Nginx) run on Belgian servers without external dependencies. No analytics. No third-party fonts. No CDN JavaScript. Cloudflare handles DDoS protection but doesn't see decrypted traffic.

**Community moderation with minimal escalation.** Moderation privileges are limited and transparent. Community members can flag content, but actual moderation decisions go through collective review. Power is distributed, not centralized.

**Forum with threaded discussions.** Discussions are structured hierarchically for readability. Threads group related conversations, making it easier to follow long-form peer support exchanges.

**Multi-language support (FR/NL/DE/EN).** All UI elements, support resources, and crisis links are translated. Regional crisis hotlines are geographically relevant—French-speaking users see French hotlines, Dutch-speaking users see Dutch resources.

**AGPL-3.0 License.** The platform is open source under AGPL-3.0, ensuring transparency. Anyone can audit the code, verify privacy claims, and deploy their own instance.

### What I Learned

**Privacy is architecture, not configuration.** You can't bolt anonymity onto an existing system. IP logging, session tracking, analytics—these are defaults in most web frameworks. Building privacy-first requires deliberate architectural choices: don't collect data you don't need, encrypt what you store, delete aggressively.

**GDPR is easier when you don't collect data.** The right to be forgotten is trivial when you never remembered. Data export is meaningless when there's nothing to export. GDPR compliance through minimization is conceptually simple but architecturally challenging—most frameworks assume data collection.

**Accessibility and security overlap.** Server-side rendering improves accessibility and reduces attack surface. Minimal JavaScript helps older devices and prevents client-side vulnerabilities. Simple authentication (pseudonym + PIN) is more accessible than OAuth flows and more secure than password recovery emails.

**Community moderation scales differently.** Centralized moderation (admins ban users) doesn't work for vulnerable communities. Distributed moderation (community reviews flags) builds trust but is slower. The trade-off: responsiveness vs. fairness.

**Self-hosting is a political choice.** Running on Belgian servers without external dependencies isn't just technical—it's about sovereignty. Users trust the platform because infrastructure can't be subpoenaed by foreign governments or accessed by third-party analytics.

If I were starting over, I'd invest earlier in **automated crisis detection.** If someone posts content indicating immediate danger, the platform should surface crisis resources proactively—without logging or tracking.
